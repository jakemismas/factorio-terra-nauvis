# Great deposits by resource, against the hotspot table

Research for the second hotspot table (`hotspots-v2.json`, `tools/build_hotspots.py emit2`), 2026-10-30 and 31, by
web search and page reads, one pass per resource. Facts and places only: no dataset ships in the mod.

How to read it:

- `confirmed`: yes, the figure and the place were seen in the named source; partial, one of them rests on a search
  snippet, a secondary source or a town point; no, not confirmed, and the table says what is missing.
- Coordinates are approximate, decimal degrees, south and west negative. An extent of 0 means none was seen.
- `map status` says where the entry stands on the second table.
- Stone is a judgement: nobody publishes a world table of quarry reserves, so its list is the great dimension-stone
  districts by output, heritage standing and fame, not a measured ranking.

Limits of this pass, all of which lower confidence:

- Web searches were capped, so copper had 13 queries, uranium 16, oil 35, iron 41 (2 of them not run), stone 47, coal 50.
- PDFs could not be read, including the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries sheets, so the
  iron and copper country tables come from the USGS MCS 2026 data release CSV and many deposit figures from snippets.
- energyinst.org and OPEC's data portal refused fetches: oil and coal country tables come through secondary copies
  of those figures, and each row's source says which. Tarim, Oman and Bohai offshore were not reached.
- The UDEPO database could not be reached, and uranium figures from the IAEA Red Book are as WNA reproduces them.
- Crude oil entries 41 and 42 came later, on 2026-10-31, for the 0.2.0 review: 12 searches and page reads.

### Caveats

- Country table source: The page reader could not decode the USGS MCS 2026 iron ore PDF (compressed binary). The table was read directly from the USGS MCS 2026 data release CSV on ScienceBase (DOI 10.5066/P1WKQ63T, MCS2026_Commodities_Data.csv), section World Mine Production and Reserves. Nothing in the country table comes from memory. The chapter's narrative text was seen only as search snippets.
- Dropped to stay within the 32 cap: Marampa, Sierra Leone (1 Bt at 32% Fe, London Mining 2013 via Wikipedia, no coordinates on that page) and Pena Colorada, Mexico (no source reached; Mexico national is 940 Mt crude reserves and 7.7 Mt usable ore in 2025 est.). Chile was added because it is a top-12 reserves country. Sangaredi excluded per the research plan.
- Meaning of confirmed: yes means the figure came from a government or survey source and the coordinate from a fetched page. Partial means the figure was seen but is company-only, secondary, dated, national or state level rather than deposit level, or snippet-only. No means no figure specific to that entry was confirmed. Strict by design, so company statutory disclosures such as LKAB and Vale are still partial.
- Map rows: 11 of the 13 existing rows sit on a Wikipedia header, mine or town coordinate (to rounding) rather than a district centroid; iron-kiruna-malmberget is instead the exact midpoint of two sites, and iron-lorraine-minette is 13 km from Briey. Rows needing attention: iron-labrador-trough (main producing cluster outside the circle), iron-kursk-magnetic-anomaly (Mikhailovsky outside, and r100 is a fraction of the quoted area), iron-mesabi-range (east end outside), iron-sishen (Kolomela town outside), iron-anshan (Benxi outside), iron-gara-djebilet (radius far too large if 131 km2 is right). Each entry's grade_or_note has a MAP CHECK line.
- Extent values: a 0 means no source gave that dimension, not that it is zero. Many extents are derived, and each derivation is stated in the entry. For multi-site rows (Iran, Urals, Kola, Mbalam-Belinga) length is only the spacing between sites, so map them as separate points, not as a filled region. Distances were computed by hand with an equirectangular approximation; treat them as accurate to a few km.
- USGS reserves mix bases. Australia is 59,000 Mt in the table but only 24 Bt crude ore is JORC-compliant or equivalent. Ukraine uses Soviet A and B categories. Geoscience Australia's EDR of 58,522 Mt agrees with the USGS headline.
- Crude ore rank is not iron rank. By iron content the order is Australia, Brazil, Russia, Mauritania, India, China, United States, Canada and Ukraine (tied), Iran and Kazakhstan (tied), Peru. China falls from 4th to 6th because 17,000 Mt crude holds only 3,000 Mt iron, and Chile drops out of the top 12.
- India's Bureau of Mines figures are reserves plus resources and are much larger than the USGS 5,500 Mt crude reserves line, so state and USGS numbers are not comparable. Yearbook editions also differ in reference date.
- Guinea, Venezuela, Liberia, Sierra Leone, Algeria, Gabon, Cameroon and Congo are not named in the MCS 2026 table. They fall in Other countries (11,000 Mt crude, 64 Mt usable ore) or are unreported. Simandou therefore has no USGS figure despite starting in November 2025.
- Coverage of the requested authorities: USGS, Geoscience Australia, NRCan, Indian Bureau of Mines and the SGB (value estimates only) were reached. No usable tonnage was reached from BGR, BGS, Geological Survey of India, SGU, BRGM, or any Russian or Chinese state survey. Russian and Chinese deposit figures rest on trade, academic, Wikipedia or old US government sources.
- PDFs from Indian Bureau of Mines, Kumba, the Grand Est administration and the WA government were seen only as search snippets. The page reader returned 403 or similar for Mining Weekly, Engineering News, mining.com, MDPI, Britannica and De Gruyter, so those figures are snippet-level. Web search summaries sometimes did not attach a figure to a specific URL; where that matters the entry says so.

### Caveats

- SOURCES NOT REACHED: nothing was read from SERNAGEOMIN, Cochilco, BGR, BGS, PGI, LNEG or IGME. PDF text does not come through this page reader, so the MCS PDFs, the ICSG Factbook PDF and the USGS assessment PDFs were unreadable. Country figures come from the USGS MCS 2026 data release CSV, which is the same data. USGS assessment figures come from abstracts on landing pages. ICSG capacities come from a Mining Visuals copy credited to The World Copper Factbook 2024, not from ICSG directly.
- AGE AND BASIS: USGS OFR 2008-1155 and OFR 2003-107 tonnages are total deposit endowment (past production plus resources) as compiled by 2008 and 2007, at mixed cut-offs. They are not current reserves and cannot be compared with MCS reserves. Sums of contained copper are my tonnage x grade arithmetic, done by hand. Some rows are clearly stale, such as Kansanshi at 3.4 Mt in OFR 2003-107.
- EXTRACTION: database rows were read through a page reader that summarises 100,000 characters at a time. OFR 2003-107 and the VMS records do not label units, so Mt and percent are inferred. Spot-check any figure before it drives a decision. USGS deposit points can be off; Nkana North Limb in OFR 2003-107 shows lon 27.175, which looks like an error, and I excluded it.
- RANKING is my judgement, blending contained copper with current output. It is not a published ranking.
- THE 32 CAP: these deposits on the must-consider list have no row. USGS OFR 2008-1155 values: Bozshakol/Boshchekul 51.817,74.183, 1,000 Mt at 0.67%; Panguna -6.317,155.5, 1,420 Mt at 0.465%; Ok Tedi -5.2,141.133, 854 Mt at 0.64%; Malanjkhand 22.017,80.717, 797 Mt at 0.83%. ### Caveats

- CONFIRMED RULE: yes means the figure, the coordinate and the extent were each seen directly, with the figure from an authoritative source. partial means at least one of those was derived, stale, from a lesser source, or only in a snippet with a problem. Every entry has at least one figure actually seen, so none is no, but many partial entries are weak: read unconfirmed_detail before using a number.
- EXTENT: extent_km of 0 means no source gave that dimension and no defensible derivation existed, it does not mean zero. Where a value is derived from a stated area with an assumed aspect ratio, or from town spacing, unconfirmed_detail says so. Town spacing values are lower bounds.
- COORDINATES: most are towns, mines or Wikipedia infobox points, not measured basin centroids. No GIS outline was used. Midpoints and distances are my own arithmetic, done by hand on a spherical approximation, not checked by a tool.
- MAP CHECK SUMMARY of the 11 existing rows. Should change: coal-nord-pas-de-calais (sits at the east end, move to about 50.44, 3.08, thin 120 by 12 km), coal-kuzbass (about 127 km too far north, centre about 54.22, 86.34, 400 by 120 km). Depends on intent: coal-witbank-highveld (misses Secunda, 71 km south), coal-jharia-damodar (misses Raniganj, 72 km east), coal-ekibastuz (r30 is about 46 times the 62 km2 deposit), coal-south-wales (circle vs a 143 by 34 km field). Fine: coal-ruhr, coal-upper-silesia, coal-donbas, coal-ordos, coal-pocahontas. Note coal-ordos's coordinate itself was not confirmed by a fetched page.
- EI ACCESS: the Energy Institute site and the BP PDF returned 403, and the KPMG copy of the 2026 review exceeded the fetch size limit. Production comes from the Our World in Data CSV built on EI 2026, read in 100,000-character chunks; latest values were summarised by the page reader, and 'unit not stated' in the CSV is taken as TWh per the grapher page. India's 17.97 EJ cross-checks against the 17.9 EJ quoted from EI 2026. Reserves labelled BP/EI end-2020 come from Planete Energies quoting BP Statistical Review 2022 (https://www.planete-energies.com/en/media/figures/global-coal-reserves). EJ values are my conversion at 0.0036 EJ per TWh.
- RESERVES: the two series differ. OWID's reserve figures are EIA data, in a CSV with no stated unit, read as tonnes. The EIA series lists Kyrgyzstan at 28.5 Bt, rank 9, which is absent from the BP/EI top 12 and looks odd; I could not explain it. EI/BP has not updated country reserves past end-2020 as far as I could see. Top 12 by reserves uses the BP/EI series, so South Africa is 12th there and 14th under EIA. Countries are listed as the union of the top-12 lists, 16 rows.
- PDFS: The page reader returned compressed binary for the EIA tables, Ministry of Coal, CMPDI/GSI inventory, Indian Bureau of Mines and Polish Geological Institute PDFs. Local copies of the PDFs were not opened. Numbers from those documents come from web search snippets of them. Reading those saved PDFs could upgrade several Indian, US and Polish figures; EIA 2024 Table 6 and the GSI coalfield table are the most valuable.
- SOURCES NOT REACHED: BGR Energy Study (bgr.bund.de connection failed, BGR values are only secondary quotes), Britannica (403), UNESCO (403), IEA main Coal 2025 had no province data. No USGS product named World Coal Assessment turned up; USGS coverage here is the National Coal Resource Assessment and the 2015 Powder River assessment. The Geological Survey of Pakistan, Council for Geoscience, MEMR and Rosnedra primaries were also not reached.
- RANKING: my judgement, by current output first, then reserves, with closed legacy rows last. Basin output and reserves are not like for like, and Indian company tonnages are not field tonnages.
- CAP OF 32: Xinjiang is one entry and Colombia is merged to fit. Considered but left out, with what was seen. Singrauli: NCL 139.00 Mt 2024-25, the largest omission by output, no coordinate fetched. South Yakutia: Elga 28.6 Mt 2024 and about 2.2 Bt reserves, Elga at 56.20, 130.64, Neryungri at 56.68, 124.67. Surat: GA EDR 7,949 Mt, Inferred 10,393 Mt. Belchatow: 44.26 Mt 2022 (GEM), 520.49 Mt reserves vs 1,930 Mt elsewhere. Maritsa East: 34.2 Mt 2022, mineable 2,096 Mt, resource 4,574 Mt, about 240 km2. Afsin-Elbistan: 3.4 to 4.9 Bt, MTA table 4,642 Mt. Zonguldak: 1,319 Mt geological, about 526 to 550 Mt proven, TTK 1.1 Mt 2022. Kosovo: 12 to 15 Bt, Sibovc 990 Mt, 7.2 Mt 2018. Karaganda: 3,600 km2, no basin output found. Pechora: Vorkutaugol 7.2 Mt 2023, no basin total. Moatize: about 11 to 11.6 Mt capacity and peak, ownership and status conflict between sources, no 2024 output. Durham and Sydney Nova Scotia: nothing usable found.
- DATA CONFLICTS LEFT OPEN: China 2025 is 4.71 Bt (IEA) vs about 4.8 Bt (official). IEA mid-year 2025 is internally inconsistent on Australia (434 Mt for 2025, then 447 Mt in 2026 described as a fall). APBI-ICMA's Xinjiang 55.29 Mt looks like a scale error. PRB Wikipedia text on Black Thunder being the top mine conflicts with a secondary summary of EIA Table 9 for 2024 that lists North Antelope Rochelle first.
- VINTAGE: this was gathered 2026-10-30. EIA's Annual Coal Report for 2025 data is due November 2026, so PRB, Illinois and Appalachian figures will soon be superseded.

### Caveats

- THIS LIST IS A JUDGEMENT. No global reserve table exists for stone: the USGS MCS 2026 dimension stone sheet lists US production only, with other countries and the world as NA. The ranking blends output, trade weight, heritage standing and map spread, and another researcher could reasonably order it differently.
- Entries are not comparable. Figures mix tonnes, m3, money, quarry counts and designations, and blocks, gross extraction and carbonate waste are different things. Carrara alone runs from 0.6 to 5 million t depending on basis. World extraction shows the same gap: gross 313 to 318 million t against about 153 to 155 million t raw and about 92 million t used.
- Country table source: Carlo Montani, XXX Rapporto Marmo e Pietre nel Mondo 2019, as cited in ICE trade agency notes, 2018 data, total 153,000. China, Italy, Egypt, Spain, Portugal, USA, Greece and Saudi Arabia come from the April 2020 note; India, Turkey, Iran and Brazil from the June 2021 note; 2020 values from the 2023 note. Montani's own tables were never seen. ICE labels the unit as million t, but the values only make sense as thousand t. India at 17% and Turkey at 7.8% check out against 153,000.
- Country table rank limit: places 1 to 6 are stated, with Italy sixth. Places 7 to 12 are only the countries I named in the query, so an unnamed country could sit between them. Egypt, Spain, Portugal, USA, Greece and Saudi Arabia are confirmed as figures, not as ranks 7 to 12.
- PDF limit: The page reader returned unreadable binary for every PDF tried (USGS MCS, ICE notes, NERC Mercian paper), and the IUGS 55 stones book exceeded the size limit. USGS, ICE, IBM, IAEG and Mercian figures were therefore seen only as search snippets, not read in the document. Saved PDFs were not opened by other means.
- Extent rule: 0 means no extent was seen. I did not guess from memory. Values for Rajsamand-Udaipur, Telangana, Verona-Botticino, Monte Coto and Drama-Thassos are town-to-town distances computed from sourced coordinates. Values for Afyon, Macael and Marmara are equal-area shapes computed from a sourced area. Most coordinates are towns, not quarry centroids.
- Industry versus quarry: Yunfu, Shuitou, Cachoeiro, Verona, Novelda and Kishangarh are processing clusters that handle imported stone as well as local stone, so they mark industry rather than a rock body. Shaq El-Thoaban was excluded for this reason: it is a processing zone in the Tora Maadi area of Cairo with no quarrying evidence and no output share found.
- Map row summary. Good centres: stone-makrana, stone-marmara-island, stone-aswan, stone-portland, stone-pentelicus. Radius looks too large: stone-portland (r10 against a 6 km island) and stone-marmara-island (r15 against 12.7 km equal-area). stone-aswan r15 is unproven. stone-indiana-limestone sits about 9.5 km south of the belt midpoint and misses Stinesville at r25. stone-carrara is on the town, with the quarries to its east. stone-pentelicus r10 could not be checked.
- Conflicts rejected or flagged: Makrana 19.20 million t per year on Wikipedia exceeds all Rajasthan marble in IBM data; Mahallat's 30 million t reserves against 2 million t per year; Macael national share of 40% versus 80%; Elberton's Granite Capital title has no statistics behind it. Rogers City's world-largest title and Monte Coto's world-largest title are claims, not rankings.
- Source quality is mixed. Many district figures come from trade press, sellers, tourism boards, press releases or Wikipedia, because national survey data by district was not found. Several are 15 to 20 years old (Afyon 2002, Porrino 2003, Denizli 2005, Sao Thome 2005). Treat them as order of magnitude.
- Near misses left out by the 26 cap, with evidence seen. Elberton, Georgia: granite mass about 35 by 6 miles, about 45 quarries, over 200,000 monuments per year (Georgia Encyclopedia and others, https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/granite/). Barre, Vermont: Smith Quarry 50 acres and 570 ft deep, no output found, tours ended 2025 (https://dec.vermont.gov/node/1942). Stanstead, Quebec: 7,000 m3 or more per year (Polycor spec sheet, https://dev2.polycor.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/StansteadGrey-Granite-SpecSheet-EN-2021-05-11.pdf). Bohus granite: outcrop 85 by 35 km, 7,000 workers at the 1929 peak (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015EGUGA..1714883S). Gallura: Rosa Beta granite designated 2019 (https://iugs-geoheritage.org/geoheritage_stones/rosa-beta-granite/). Bath stone: GHSR July 2019. Kolmarden serpentine marble: GHSR January 2019. Kota stone: over 80 mines and about 1,000 units at Ramganj Mandi (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramganj_Mandi).
- Considered and not included for lack of evidence. Caen, Oppdal and Alta: the combined searches returned nothing on them. Bangalore: no volume found. Hubei: only a trade-site share of about 18% of Chinese marble split with Shandong and Guangdong; Macheng not found. Isfahan and Yazd: no district data; Mahallat stands in. Vietnam (Luc Yen in Yen Bai, Quy Hop in Nghe An): no current tonnage. Saudi Arabia: national 1,300 thousand t but no district data. Shandong Laizhou surfaced as a possible large Chinese district with no figure. Wiesbaden excluded per the research plan.

### Caveats

- Red Book edition: a newer edition than the planned one exists. Uranium 2026 (31st edition, published September 2026, data as of 1 January 2025) is used throughout. Its PDF exceeded the fetch size limit and could not be read directly, so every Red Book figure here is as reproduced by WNA. The Uranium 2024 and 2022 country tables were not obtained (the NEA 2022 page returned 403 and its PDF host did not resolve).
- UDEPO: no deposit-level data could be reached. infcis.iaea.org returned only its About page, which says the map is a snapshot from early 2017 and that the database was renamed UThDEPO in 2026. IAEA conference and TECDOC PDFs returned 402 or 403. Only search snippets were seen: 62,674,137 tU across 2,755 deposits in 2017, and 14,034,700 tU conventional including Olympic Dam. No ranking in this file comes from UDEPO.
- Ranking is my judgement across resources, current output and history. It is not a single metric from any source.
- extent_km convention: a source-stated dimension where one was seen (noted in the entry), otherwise the spread of located sites or a stated area drawn as a square. A value of 0 means no source supported a number, not that the feature is a point. Extents were never taken from memory.
- Cross-page conflicts in WNA are unresolved. Niger 351,100 tU (Supply) against 289,606 tU (Niger page). Brazil 167,800 tU against 231,000 tU. Ukraine 161,300 tU against 184,474 tU. Uzbekistan 2024 output 4,000 tU against 5,200 tU. US 2025 output 534 tU against 831 tU. The country table uses the Supply and Production pages throughout. The differences may reflect in situ against recoverable tonnes, but no source says so.
- Other cross-source differences. Australia: Red Book 1,701,600 tU to USD 130/kgU; Geoscience Australia 1,959 ktU total identified, 1,280 ktU EDR, 2024 output 4,656 tU against WNA 4,598 tU; the WNA Australia page says about 1.2 million tU. Canada: NRCan more than 588,000 tU below USD 50/lb, 14,327 t in 2024, against WNA 551,700 tU and 14,309 tU. World 2024 output: WNA 60,213 tU, Red Book 61,924 tU. China 2024: WNA 1,600 tU estimated, Red Book 1,956 tU.
- Units: many deposit figures are in tonnes U3O8, not tU. They were left in the source's unit, and 1 t U3O8 is about 0.848 tU. Country table rows are in tU.
- Most coordinates come from Wikipedia, as the research plan allowed, and are not surveyed. Rejected: Wikipedia's Husab Mine coordinate (-22.767,14.517), which contradicts that page's own text; Cameco's Inkai page pin (48.02,66.92), which is a Kazakhstan map centre; and Wikipedia's Witwatersrand Basin coordinate (-26.20,28.04), which is the Johannesburg ridge and not the basin.
- Considered and left out to stay within 26 entries, cut on combined size and current output, with Khiagda and Beverley-Four Mile kept ahead of them as active producers. Kintyre, Australia: -22.338,122.072, 25,274 t U3O8 indicated at 0.62% (December 2015, WNA), 60 to 70 km south of Telfer depending on source. Caetite and Lagoa Real, Brazil: town -14.069,-42.475, about 10,000 tU at 0.3%, undated, the only operating Brazilian mine (WNA). - Tumas, Dasa, Imouraren, Budenovskoye, Inkai, Tortkuduk and Katco are covered inside their district entries, not as separate entries, because each sits within a district already listed.
